Avoiding dental decay

December 15, 2025 · Family Dental Clinic

Dental decay, also called caries, holes or cavities, happens when teeth are not cleaned well. The formula is simple: bacteria + sugar + time = cavities. Break the chain and you protect your teeth.

Seven habits that keep cavities away

  1. Clean up soon after meals. Within about 15 minutes of eating, floss for a minute, then brush for at least two minutes.
  2. Rinse with water after eating and drinking to wash away sugars and food particles.
  3. Brush twice a day for at least two minutes, using gentle circular movements.
  4. Floss every day, including just under the gum line where your brush cannot reach.
  5. Cut down on sugary drinks and snacks. Less sugar means less food for the bacteria that cause decay.
  6. Eat mineral-rich foods such as dairy and vegetables, which help keep teeth strong.
  7. See a dental professional regularly for scaling and polishing, which limits the spread of bacteria and decay.

Already have a toothache?

Pain, sensitivity to sweet or cold, or a visible dark spot can all be signs of a cavity. Caught early, a cavity can be fixed with a simple filling. Left alone, it can spread to the nerve and need a root canal or extraction.
